# Guardrail sharing and embedded Policy content

A Guardrail read share does not grant access to a private Policy referenced by that Guardrail. The same rule applies to frozen Policy definitions, generated tests, compiled artifacts and Policy-derived failure details embedded in Guardrail versions and reports.

For read-only Guardrail recipients, draft expectation overrides and excluded case IDs are also withheld unless a matching readable frozen Policy snapshot establishes source access. An observation-only expectation can copy a Policy's original Test Case into its expected output, so redacting the frozen version alone is insufficient. The Guardrail owner and platform administrator retain the owner-authored mutable draft; response filtering does not modify the stored draft or runtime configuration.

Tenant-facing version views expose source content only when the frozen Policy leaves match currently readable Policy versions, or exact versions of the shipped public catalog, and the version's plan and artifact agree with those leaves. A deleted or reused Policy identity cannot establish historical access by itself. Unknown or malformed provenance is denied or redacted. Internal compilation and Runner distribution retain the stored definitions; response redaction must not modify them.

Artifact validation checks the publication/import structure and plan correspondence. It relies on the stored signed publication contract for compiled content integrity; these read paths do not recompile arbitrary YAML or Colang to prove semantic equivalence.

Package export is an onward redistribution operation. A tenant export requires exact currently owned Policy content or exact shipped catalog content. A read-only Policy share permits reading that Policy, but does not permit redistributing it in a Guardrail package. Consequently, an owned Guardrail that embeds another tenant's shared Policy can still be refused for export. Missing/deleted/reused Policy origins and malformed leaf/plan/artifact correspondence fail closed. Platform administrators retain their authority, while a package must remain semantically valid to be exported.

These checks can make historical content unavailable when its original ownership was never retained. Recovering that ownership requires reliable evidence; an old resource ID alone is insufficient. Durable frozen-origin ownership, audited remediation and request-wide atomic revocation remain future work. Current read checks do not lock an entire request against concurrent changes in ownership or shares.

Copying a shared Guardrail and reading a raw draft diff require independent source access too. Otherwise a recipient could copy hidden expectation overrides into a new owned Guardrail, or obtain their text through the diff. A Guardrail-only recipient is refused when any pinned/generated Policy reference lacks matching readable frozen provenance; a published copy must prove its own frozen source. Unfrozen drafts composed entirely from shipped public catalog Policies remain copyable. Owners and platform administrators retain their authored snapshot behavior. Copies legitimately made while source access was granted remain owned copies after revocation; revocation cannot retract content already disclosed. Previously created copies with missing or unsafe provenance require separate historical review.
